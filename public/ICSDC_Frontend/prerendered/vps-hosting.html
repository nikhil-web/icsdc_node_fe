VPS Hosting : FAQs

01Is my CPU/RAM truly dedicated, or is it burstable?
Your resources are truly guaranteed and dedicated via ICSDC KVM virtualization. There is no resource over-selling, ensuring your assigned CPU, RAM, and storage IOPS are consistently available to your environment.
02Can I install a custom OS or kernel?
Yes. You are given full root access, allowing you complete freedom to install any compatible Linux distribution or Windows OS version, and to compile or modify custom kernel modules as your application demands.
03How fast is the VPS provisioning process?
Provisioning is instant and automated. Once your order is processed, your VPS environment will be available and ready for SSH access within minutes, allowing you to deploy your applications immediately.
04What is the process for scaling up resources?
Scaling is on-demand and vertical. You can instantly add CPU cores, RAM, or storage via your central control panel with minimal to zero downtime, eliminating the need for complex server migration.
05Do you offer management services?
ICSDC provides unmanaged VPS hosting, giving you maximum control and cost efficiency. We manage the hardware and network, while you manage the OS, software, and application stack using your full root access.
06How is VPS Hosting different from Shared Hosting?
With shared hosting, your resources are pooled with other websites on the same server. With VPS Hosting, your CPU, RAM, and storage are ring-fenced and guaranteed - no other user's traffic spike affects your performance.
07Can I upgrade my VPS plan later without downtime?
Yes. Vertical scaling lets you add CPU, RAM, or storage directly from your control panel, with minimal to zero downtime - no migration to a new server required.
08Is VPS Hosting suitable for WordPress or e-commerce sites?
Yes VPS is a strong fit once a site outgrows shared hosting, particularly for WordPress sites with plugin-heavy setups or e-commerce stores expecting variable traffic, since you get dedicated resources rather than shared capacity.
